My Story

In high school, I failed my 12th board exams, and that failure changed everything for me.

I was a PCM student. Everyone around me was moving forward. I was stuck. I still remember that silence at home. Not anger, just worry.

My parents could have forced me into repeat coaching immediately. They did not. They knew I liked to question, to read, to build. My father did something brave. He took away all academic books and gave me one year. No syllabus. No exam. He said, find what you actually love.

So for one year, I was free. Imagine having a full library and nobody asking for marks. I read philosophy late at night. Science magazines in the morning. Random books on brain, universe, life. Nobody to judge. Slowly I stopped reading to pass, and started reading to understand.

One thing kept coming back. I love physics. But I do not want physics only on paper. I want it to touch someone's life. A patient. A hospital. Healthcare. That one line gave me direction when I had nothing.

Next year I came back to boards, JEE, IAT like everyone else. Same books, different girl. This time I knew why I was studying. I cleared them and reached IISER-TVM. First week there, I looked around and thought, these people are curious like me. They want to know how things work. That pushed me more than any rank.

After that I just kept exploring. Quantum optics in Roorkee. Bioinformatics and plasmonics in Delhi. EEG and consciousness with Surrey. Microrobots in Stuttgart. Biosensors for colon cancer in Kiel. Batteries, cells, brains, sensors. Different labs, same question: how can science help a human body?

I am still that girl from the gap year. Curious, hardworking, a little stubborn. I think I found my ikigai, and I want to go deeper.

From that gap year to IISER to labs across the world, I am still following the same question I found at 17.

About

Driven physics student working across experimental sensing, microfluidics, quantum theory, electrochemistry and computational biology. My recent work focuses on non-invasive magnetic nanoparticle (MNP) detection for biomedical sensing using surface-acoustic-wave (SAW) magnetoelastic and Delta-E magnetoelectric sensors. including measurements through biological tissue for early detection of complications after gastrointestinal cancer surgery.

In parallel I work on electromagnetic field theories of consciousness with EEG analysis (University of Surrey), magnetic microrobots in microfluidic channels (University of Stuttgart), quantum optical Bloch equations (IIT Roorkee), sodium-ion battery cathodes (IISER TVM), and bioinformatics tool development in R/Shiny (University of Delhi).

Currently I am doing my master thesis under Prof. Deepshikha Jaiswal Nagar at IISER-TVM. I am learning MOF synthesis and I want to understand its magnetic properties.

Solo · Greece

Athens on foot: history you can touch

I went to Athens solo, with no fixed plan. Just walked, watched, and let the city talk.

Athens is full of history and culture. Greece is home to great physicists, mathematicians and philosophers, and you feel that everywhere. A Greek shop lady told me something I never knew. Pythagoras was a great philosopher, not just a mathematician. She even showed me the special Pythagorean wine glass he made for his students. Such a small shop, such a big story.

I tried Greek coffee. There is a whole different way of drinking it. You sip it slowly, and it slowly settles at the bottom. No rush. I tried the local food too, simple and full of taste.

I made random friends. Some from the hostel, some locals. Someone told me Greece is made of around 2000 islands. I had no idea.

One evening I just walked up to Acropolis hill, sat there, and watched the sunset. That quiet moment, alone above a 2500 year old city, was another level of joy.

Funny thing: Greece was the only place where the letters around me felt familiar. Alpha, beta, gamma, pi, theta. I had written them a thousand times in maths and physics, and suddenly they were on shop boards and street signs.

Solo trips always teach me something. Travelling makes me feel alive. Life feels like it is moving, and I am not stuck somewhere. Athens was amazing.

Amsterdam

Amsterdam: the city I fell in love with

I do not even know where to start. What a city. What a vibe. What culture, what people, what food. Everything about Amsterdam is just outstanding. It is so lively and so beautiful. I literally fell in love with that city.

That city has made something feel very normal which is actually not that normal, especially coming from India. Acceptance of same sex relationships. I was randomly walking near a canal by a bridge, and two girls were getting married. People on a boat passing below were cheering for them. It was pride month. Witnessing that joy, so open and so celebrated, was something wonderful. I had never seen that before.

In the evening I sat near a canal eating a stroopwafel. Simple, sweet, perfect. I also walked through the Red Light district. It was full of people. I learned that sex workers there get government benefits and police are present the whole time for their safety. It showed me how a city can respect every profession. I cannot even explain how wonderful that trip was.

I also went to the Rijksmuseum. It was full of beautiful paintings by very famous artists. One of them was Van Gogh. I saw Van Gogh's self portrait in person. He used to paint fully with lines, building the whole picture from strokes. Standing in front of the actual painting and seeing those lines up close was a wonderful experience. There was a lot more there too, but that moment stayed with me.

Amsterdam taught me what an open city feels like. I left a piece of my heart there.

Solo · Belgium

Bruges: my first solo trip, straight into a fairy tale

Bruges was my first solo trip in Europe. And the moment I saw the city, I just thought, what is this fairy tale town?

On the way there I was on a FlixBus, and I met a Brazilian lady who was also travelling alone. We started talking, and then came the coincidence. We were staying in the same hostel. So we ended up exploring together for almost one and a half days.

That is when I learned something. A solo trip is not always solo. It is about leaving your comfort zone, talking to strangers, and meeting people you would never meet otherwise.

In Belgium we explored churches, the market square, and all the beautiful corners of Bruges. And how can I forget the Belgian waffle? If you are in Belgium, waffle is a must. It was amazing. And the Belgian beer was next level.

At sunset the canals looked so pretty. We just sat near one canal with our drinks and watched the light change. That quiet moment was wonderful. The boat ride was special too. The guide explained the history of the places as we floated past, through Bruges and towards Ghent.

Bruges and Ghent are both so pretty. Sitting near a castle, reading a book, horse carts moving around you. It felt like a different century. Bruges is a very small city. To go from one place to another, you either walk, take a boat, take a horse carriage, or ride a bike. We literally walked everywhere, exploring lanes, making friends in the hostel.

It was a beautiful experience. My first solo trip taught me that going alone does not mean being alone.
